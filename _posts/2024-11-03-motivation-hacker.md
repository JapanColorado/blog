## *The Motivation Hacker* - Notes

## General Method

1. Pick your goals
2. Figure out the subtasks that lead to those goals
3. Figure out which motivation hacks to use on those subtasks
4. Use far more of them than you need

## The basis for motivation hacking

### *__Motivation__* = *Expectancy* **Value* / (*Impulsiveness** *Delay*)

- *Expectancy* - Confidence of success
- *Value* - Reward level and importance
- *Impulsiveness* - Likelihood of doing something else
- *Delay* - Distance from reward/punishment

### Mindsets

- *Expectancy* - No matter the task, you know in your core that you can learn to do it well, so you look forward to any challenge.
- *Value* - You spend most of your time doing things so fulfilling that whenever you stop and think about it, you can’t help but grin to yourself.
- *Impulsiveness* - At any time, there’s only one clear thing that you want to do, so you have no problems focusing on it.
- *Delay* - You are always so close to achieving one goal or another that you never lack the urge to go finish something.

## A toolbox of motivation hacks

### Maximizing Expectancy & Value

- *Success Spirals*
  - Increasing your Expectancy by creating a chain of small successes.
  - Similar to streaks, but with an end date.
  - Steps:
    1. Set an easy goal with a completion date that is based on inputs rather than outputs
    2. Plan for worst case scenario and adjust rate accordingly, or set bad outcomes as explicit excuses
    3. Track success and never weasel out of doing the goal
    4. Gradually increase the difficulty of spirals
- *The Sword of Last Chance*
  - DANGER! This is a Hail Mary strategy for those stuck at a dark pit of broken success spirals.
  - This is the last chance for change. Swing the sword, cut away neuroses, decide which success spiral to grow(make sure it has a time limit), and use as many motivation hacks as possible. If you fail now, you’ve failed forever.
  - Prove you can change. Do 1 thing. Maybe flossing. Maybe exercising. Make it easy enough that on your worst possible day you can still follow through. Set a short deadline, maybe 2 weeks, and follow through.
  - If you are truly in an awful place to start change, set it aside and wait. But do not delay long.  A deadly excuse is not the same as a true reason. You know the difference.
  - The motivation comes not from fear of failure, but from the confidence that you’ll follow through on your goals(Expectancy).
- Over-precommitment
  - Hacking Expectancy in the present by minimizing your possible Impulsiveness in the future.
  - The stakes should be much higher than the bare minimum needed for motivation.
  - Steps:
    1. Set up a precommitment device.
    2. Whatever stakes you attached, make them more extreme. For example, if $10 to your parents motivates you, make it $100 to an organization you hate instead.
    3. NEVER WEASEL! Do not make the stakes so extreme that you’ll weasel out if you fail, or find a way to guarantee you’ll follow through on the punishment if you fail.
    4. Potentially use many different commitment devices, eg. money, social media, accountability partners, etc.

### Minimizing Impulsiveness & Delay

- Precommitment/Commitment Devices
  - Choosing now to limit your options later, preventing the wrong choice when faced with temptation.
  - Reduces Impulsiveness in the moment by bringing the long-term consequences of not following through into the present.
  - Steps:
    1. Set up a success spiral
    2. Decide the level of precommitment that the goal requires, eg. quitting smoking will need more extreme precommitment than not eating desert for a week.
    3. Add stakes to not following your goal, eg. losing money or having to tell a friend you failed
    4. Use a service that makes it harder to weasel, eg. Beeminder or StickK, or use an accountability partner(or both!).
    5. Make the stakes about the daily actions, not the outcome, eg. not eating cake vs. losing 10 pounds.
-Burnt Ships
  - Disabling, removing, or destroying a distraction to limit potential impulsiveness.
  - Examples including disabling the internet until lunch or blocking distracting websites.
  - Counts as a type of precommitment, but with the added benefit of actually removing potential distractions.

## Exercises for picking goals

- Motivation hackers are in danger of achieving the wrong goals because they can hack their motivation for any goal.
- Imagine your ideal day. What do you do? Who do you talk to? Where do you go? Pick goals that bring you closer to this ideal.
- Rate your potential goals on the following three factors, plug into  $\frac{e\cdot P(s)}{t}$, and sort from high to low
    1. $e$ - How much the goal excites you
    2. $P(s)$ - Your probability of success if you tried as hard as you could()
    3. $t$ - How long it would take in hours
- Imagine you’re a different person more competent than yourself that was just dropped into your life with none of your current obligations but all your current predicaments. Forget what has come before. What would you do?
- Turn into CSI-Approach goals
    1. Challenging - Should be exciting and have Value
    2. Specific - No room for Impulsiveness, you know what to do to make progress
    3. Immediate - Start now and finish soon
    4. Approach - Contrasted with Avoidance goals, Approach goals are what you do instead of what you don’t
- Don’t pick goals from what society wants you to do.
- Don’t pick goals based on childhood dreams that no longer excite you.
- Bad goals are often in the form of intermediate steps.
    1. If you want to be rich, don’t go to college, battle debt, and then climb your way up to being rich.
    2. Run before you walk.
- Continuing to do things you’re already good at can be good, but be careful you’re doing them for the right reasons.
- Goals that are too easy won’t excite you.

## General Notes

### Willpower & general motivation

- Willpower - Aptitude at setting up personal rules at whim and following them.
- Willpower should not be relied on for attaining goals besides when deciding to begin.
- If you truly cannot commit now, pick a date far enough in the future so that it’s not as scary.
- Don’t force yourself to work on your goals
  - No reliance on willpower, overconfidence, or self-doubt about motivation hacking
- If you have a valueless task you need to do, make a game out of it so that it challenges you.
- Surround yourself with motivated people.
- Feeling energized makes everything easier. Optimize your energy levels.

### Self-improvement

- When you’re wasting time, increase the difficulty by adding more pursuits.
- If you’re competing with people whose life isn’t as full as yours, you’ll have to work extra hard to keep up. They can devote their entire life to that one area.
- Collect fun-dense activities and do those instead of wimpy leisure distraction.
- Use a good task management system
- Improve by using small and rigorous self experiments
- Avoiding the planning fallacy and make your success spirals absolutely tiny
- Flooding:
  - Exposing yourself to a terrifying stimulus over and over until you get over it and realize there was nothing to be afraid of.
  - Basis behind exposure therapy and rejection therapy

### Learning

- Learning should make you smile.
- Focus on the learning process, not the end goal.
- Make sure you are learning things that are useful or fun(high Value).
- Strategy for learning skills:
  1. Get excited about a skill.
  2. While you’re excited, make time and hack up motivation to practice it.
  3. Learn how to practice it from reading or from a teacher.
  4. Start doing it right away.
- Use Anki to learn facts, avoiding the mistakes and following the approach outlined [here](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/twenty-rules-of-formulating-knowledge).

## My thoughts

It's been a while since I've read *The Motivation Hacker*. I don't use much of what Winter lays out in the book. Winter's lifestyle seems rather exhausting and idiosyncratic to me. Self improvement is definitely a big part of my life, but it seems like it is the majority of Winter's life. I'm sure Winter would disagree with that, and this book is pretty exclusively self-improvement centered, so that perception is probably skewed. Definitely take it with a kernel of pepper.

I think his theory of motivation about success spirals and expectancy might be correct, but it doesn't seem useful to think that every failure is potentially irrecoverable. I do think it's helpful to take the reduced self-confidence into account when you're thinking about the negative consequences of flaking out on yourself.

I do use Beeminder as my primary tool for keeping myself on track with my goals, but I'm currently experimenting with Beeminding a minimal amount of goals and trusting myself with more leeway and free time.

As a sidenote, I'd be curious how much of this book Winter still agrees with. If I wrote a book *right now*, on *any topic*, I would expect to disagree with at least 50% of it 5 years later with about 90% confidence.

All in all, this book was partially useful and partially, *woah, this guy's weird even by my standards.* I'm glad if Winter is enjoying his life, and if you get value from any of his advice, then that's great. Personally, I don't think that Winter's life is the one I want to aspire to.
